Persistence testing

A change must remain before it becomes a finding.

Short-lived noise, transitions, and isolated exceptions are not presented the same way as divergence that remains across time, observations, or relevant operating cycles.

Signal integrity

Data quality is kept separate from physical behavior.

The review should show whether evidence may reflect the system, the instrumentation, or the data path.

Physical-behavior review

Relationship change

Signals are available and timely, but their operating relationship has moved away from the established baseline.

  • Pressure-to-flow response changed
  • Demand-to-output response weakened
  • Behavior persisted in the same mode
Data-quality review

Instrumentation or timing issue

The available evidence may be affected by the measurement or data path and is identified for separate review.

  • Stale, repeated, or flat values
  • Missing intervals or timestamp shifts
  • Possible drift, scaling change, or disagreement

Historical assessment and live monitoring

Start historically. Move live only when justified.

01

Historical assessment

Use an approved baseline and comparison period from one system. Review findings against known operating context, interventions, and repairs.

  • Bounded data transfer
  • No live connection required
  • Known events can be compared
02

Live monitoring

If historical results are useful, connect an approved read-only source and continue the same evidence and human-review workflow.

  • Read-only ingestion
  • Persistent-change review
  • Engineering authority remains unchanged

Human in the loop

An additional set of eyes for engineering teams.

Neraium never controls equipment. It surfaces changes and supporting relationships. Engineers remain in control. They validate the context, inspect the system, and decide what happens next.

What Neraium does not do
  • Control equipment
  • Change operating setpoints
  • Diagnose root cause
  • Replace existing alarms
  • Replace engineering judgment
