Analysis layer

Relationships first, then persistence.

01

Relationship analysis

Individual readings are evaluated in the context of related signals: pump demand against flow response, pressure against flow, valve command against temperature response, and other relevant operating relationships.

02

Operational-mode awareness

Schedules, staging, demand ranges, maintenance periods, and known operating modes provide context so expected transitions are not treated like unexplained behavior.

03

Persistence testing

Short-lived noise and transient changes are separated from divergence that remains across time, observations, or relevant operating cycles.

Evidence layer

Signal integrity

Data quality is kept separate from physical behavior.

An engineering review needs to know whether the evidence may reflect the system, the instrumentation, or the data path.

Physical-behavior review

Relationship change

Signals are present and timely, but their operating relationship has moved away from the baseline.

  • Pressure-to-flow response changed
  • Demand-to-output response weakened
  • Behavior persisted within the same mode
Data-quality review

Instrumentation or timing issue

The available evidence may be affected by the measurement or data path and is identified for separate review.

  • Stale or repeated values
  • Missing intervals or timestamp shifts
  • Possible sensor drift or scaling change

Assessment paths

Start historically. Move live only when justified.

01

Historical assessment

Use an approved baseline and comparison period from one chilled-water or hydronic system. Review the resulting findings with the engineering team.

  • Bounded data transfer
  • Known events and repairs can be compared
  • No live connection required
02

Live monitoring

If the historical review is useful, connect an approved read-only source and continue the same evidence and human-review workflow.

  • Read-only ingestion
  • Persistent-change review
  • Engineering team retains authority

Human in the loop

An extra set of eyes, not an autonomous control system.

Neraium surfaces changes and supporting relationships. Engineers validate the context, inspect the system, and decide what happens next.

  • Read-only by design
  • Never controls equipment
  • Evidence remains visible
  • Engineers remain in control
