Relevant physical behavior

One connected cooling loop

A pump circulates water from the central plant to the building cooling load and back. Power is the effort; delivered flow and differential pressure describe the hydraulic response.

More power for comparable delivery may warrant investigation even when individual readings remain within limits.

Existing signals
Pump power, speed, runtime, delivered flow, and differential pressure.

Review related signals together. An isolated power reading cannot establish whether hydraulic behavior changed.

Operating-context considerations

Compare periods with similar cooling load, equipment staging, commanded speed, and pressure target. Changes in these conditions can change the relationship without establishing degradation.

Incomplete comparable history may instead yield insufficient evidence. Partial comparability must remain explicit.

In a real finding, inspect these recorded items before relying on the observation. This illustration supplies no measured windows, sample counts, confidence levels, or quantified consequence.

Limitations and engineering review

Known limitations

Telemetry alone cannot determine root cause or distinguish hydraulic restriction from pump-performance degradation. Behavioral change does not establish failure timing.

Check signal quality and the comparison context first. Engineering reviews the evidence and determines whether further analysis or a field check is warranted.
