Four-step evaluation

Bounded from the beginning.

The scope, data boundary, review question, and decision criteria are agreed before analysis begins.

  1. 01

    Select one system

  2. 02

    Provide an approved historical baseline and comparison period

  3. 03

    Review findings with the engineering team

  4. 04

    Decide whether live monitoring is justified

Data readiness

What the assessment needs.

A focused export is often enough to begin. A live connection is not required.

System boundary

Asset and signal list

The selected system, relevant equipment, available tags, units, and known signal limitations.

Baseline

Representative operation

A period that includes the normal modes and demand ranges relevant to the review question.

Comparison

A period worth reviewing

Later operation, a known change, a maintenance interval, or another approved comparison window.

Context

Known operating history

Schedules, staging, maintenance, repairs, events, and operator notes when available.

Pilot outputs

What the engineering team receives.

01 / Scope

System and data map

The approved boundary, mapped signals, operating context, exclusions, and data-quality observations.

02 / Baseline

Relationship baseline

A technical view of how relevant signals behaved together in the selected normal period.

03 / Findings

Evidence review

Persistent changes with first detection, supporting relationships, context, and post-intervention behavior when available.

04 / Decision

Assessment closeout

A documented review of what was useful, what was not, and whether live monitoring deserves further consideration.

Review standard

A finding must be useful to an engineer.

The pilot is not judged by the number of findings. It is judged by whether the evidence is understandable, technically reviewable, and relevant enough to improve where investigation begins.

Closeout questions
  • Did the evidence identify a real behavioral change?
  • Was the timing credible?
  • Were supporting relationships understandable?
  • Were data-quality issues separated clearly?
  • Did the finding improve investigation context?
  • Would continued monitoring justify the effort?
