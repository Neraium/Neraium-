Historical Evaluation

Start with history, not a live connection.

A Historical Evaluation determines whether approved telemetry can support one bounded question, establish a usable operating reference, and support a conclusion about persistent change.

The first conversation is about fit and scope. No telemetry, credentials, diagrams, network details, or sensitive operating information are needed to determine whether an evaluation makes sense.

INITIAL OPERATING BOUNDARY
  • No live connection initially
  • No administrative credentials
  • No control-system changes
  • No operational data through the public form

Scope and a customer-approved transfer method are agreed before telemetry is exchanged.

Evaluation process

One system. One question. Five disciplined steps.

  1. 01

    Define the review question

    Set the bounded behavior the team wants to understand.

  2. 02

    Scope available telemetry

    Agree signals, coverage, context, limitations, and transfer boundary.

  3. 03

    Establish a usable operating reference

    Qualify history where the data supports one.

  4. 04

    Evaluate persistent change

    Compare later behavior under relevant conditions and time scales.

  5. 05

    Review the outcome

    Discuss evidence and limitations with human judgment in control.

Responsible outcomes

A bounded answer, not a promised finding.

The evaluation states what the data can and cannot support. Engineering decides what happens next.

When a qualified operating reference is established, later approved telemetry can support a scoped Continued Analysis. Live integration is not required.

Data suitability

Can telemetry support the question and a usable reference?

Supported finding

Is meaningful persistent change supported with evidence and limitations?

Insufficient evidence

Is the responsible conclusion that the available data cannot answer?
