Historical Evaluation

Start with history, not a live connection.

A Historical Evaluation uses approved telemetry from one bounded system to establish whether the data can support a meaningful review of operating behavior.

INITIAL OPERATING BOUNDARY
  • No live connection initially
  • No administrative credentials
  • No control-system changes
  • No operational data through the public form

Scope and transfer method are agreed before any telemetry is exchanged.

Evaluation process

One system. One question. Five disciplined steps.

  1. 01

    Define the review question

    Describe the bounded system behavior the engineering or operations team wants to understand.

  2. 02

    Scope the system and available telemetry

    Review approved signals, time coverage, operating context, and data limitations before transfer.

  3. 03

    Evaluate historical operating behavior

    Assess data quality and signal usability, establish a suitable baseline, and learn expected relationships and context.

  4. 04

    Determine whether persistent change is supported

    Compare later behavior across comparable conditions and relevant time scales.

  5. 05

    Review findings and limitations together

    Discuss a supported finding or an explicit insufficient-evidence outcome with human engineering judgment in control.

Before transfer

Fit and scope come first.

The initial conversation stays at a practical level. Neraium does not need telemetry, facility diagrams, credentials, network information, or sensitive operating details to discuss whether an evaluation may fit.

Responsible outcomes

A finding is possible, not promised.

A Historical Evaluation may support a persistent-change finding, identify limitations that narrow the conclusion, or determine that the available telemetry is insufficient. Each is a valid technical result.
